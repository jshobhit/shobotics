Svaram / Sonorium Experience StudyResearch intention
01

From experience
to researchable evidence.

Svaram has an opportunity to systematically document what happens during a Sonorium sound experience, through both the participant’s subjective experience and measurable physiological changes.

The October study will establish a simple, repeatable framework for collecting three synchronized forms of information.

Layer A

Physiology

Continuous heart-rate and beat-to-beat cardiac data during the sound experience.

Layer B

Sound environment

A timestamped auditory recording, allowing physiological changes to be aligned with specific moments within the session.

Layer C

Participant experience

A structured participant assessment of physical, emotional and mental state before and after the experience, with qualitative responses.

Primary research question

What measurable physiological and self-reported changes occur during and immediately following a Sonorium sound experience?

Secondary exploratory questions

  • How do heart rate and heart-rate variability change through the session?
  • Are particular periods associated with observable physiological transitions?
  • How does perceived physical, emotional and mental state change from before to after the session?
  • Do subjective changes correspond with physiological changes?
  • Are there recurring patterns across participants?
  • Which participant-experience dimensions appear most sensitive to the Sonorium experience?

The immediate objective is a high-quality exploratory dataset from which stronger research questions and future controlled studies can emerge. The pilot will not establish therapeutic efficacy.

Protocol decisions before collection

Svaram / Sonorium Experience StudyMeasurement framework
03

Three complementary data layers.

A / Physiological layer

Cardiac response over time

The proposed wearable is the Polar H10 chest strap. Continuous measurements will allow extraction of:

  • Heart rate and RR intervals
  • RMSSD and SDNN
  • Short-window HRV trends
  • Baseline-to-session changes
  • Session-to-recovery changes

RR intervals measure the time between heartbeats. RMSSD and SDNN are time-domain measures of heart-rate variability.

Research comparing the H10 with laboratory ECG reports close agreement for time-domain HRV in healthy young adults. See validation study. Recording windows and quality checks will be defined in the protocol.

B / Sound layer

A shared reference timeline

A room microphone will capture a continuous timestamped reference recording of the sound experience.

Preserving synchronized audio makes later investigation possible around:

  • Instrument transitions and silence
  • Intensity changes and rhythmic sections
  • Sustained tones and frequency characteristics
  • Facilitator-defined events

Sophisticated acoustic analysis is outside the immediate October requirement. Audio provides a reference for linking sound events with physiological change.

One connected view. Physiology, sound and participant experience are interpreted together using a common session timeline.

C / Participant experience assessment

The human-facing research interface

The questionnaire will use short, understandable questions to explore physical, emotional and mental experience. Plain-language prompts will make the assessment accessible and comfortable to complete.

Before the session

  • Physical comfort, tension and pain / bodily discomfort
  • Breathing awareness and perceived nervous-system activation
  • Emotional state, stress / calmness and mood
  • Mental activity and focus / clarity
  • Fatigue / energy and sleep quality
  • Relevant contextual factors

After the session

Repeat comparable measures, adding:

  • Perceived physical change and relaxation / release
  • Emotional shifts and changes in mental activity
  • Awareness of breath / body
  • Unusual or significant sensations
  • Moments that felt important
  • Open-ended description of the experience

Most quantitative questions will use a 0–10 scale, with plain-language anchors and a “Not sure / Can’t tell” option. This provides structured quantitative data alongside richer qualitative material. Final wording and domain mapping will be agreed during questionnaire development.

One connected participant record.

The resulting dataset will preserve the sequence of each experience and link its physiological, auditory and self-reported dimensions.

This gives Svaram a dataset that can support several levels of future research, beyond a one-time questionnaire study.

Initial analysis plan

The first analysis will be descriptive and exploratory.

Physiological

Describe the trajectories

  • Baseline vs session HR / HRV
  • Baseline vs recovery
  • Within-session trajectories
  • Participant-to-participant variability
Subjective

Describe reported change

  • Pre / post score changes
  • Experience-domain changes
  • Distribution of reported effects
  • Recurring qualitative themes
Multimodal

Explore relationships

  • Physiological change ↔ subjective change
  • Subsequently, sound events ↔ physiological change
  • Patterns to inform future hypotheses

Interpretation grounded in data quality

Review recording completeness, signal artefacts and comparable analysis windows before interpretation. Report the number of usable records and missing responses alongside findings. These exploratory associations will not support causal or clinical claims.

06

A shared study.
Clear ownership.

Shobotics owns the rig’s hardware and software intellectual property and the questionnaire format. The study data belongs jointly to Shobotics and Svaram.

Shobotics

Platform intellectual property

Shobotics retains ownership of the hardware design and software developed for the research rig, together with the questionnaire format used in the study.

Use of these tools in the collaboration does not transfer their intellectual property to Svaram.

Shobotics + Svaram

Jointly owned study data

The data generated by the study belongs jointly to Shobotics and Svaram. This includes participant responses, physiological recordings, session audio and the consolidated research dataset.

Access and research use will respect participant consent and the agreed data-handling protocol.

Research collaboration in practice

Shobotics and Svaram will agree how study data is stored, accessed and used for analysis, publication and subsequent research. The protocol will distinguish the jointly owned data from the underlying Shobotics platform and questionnaire format.

Svaram / Sonorium Experience StudyScope & longer-term opportunity
07

The first study.
A foundation for the next.

The October phase is an exploratory research and feasibility study, designed to establish a repeatable way to turn Sonorium experiences into structured, comparable and researchable data.

What this phase establishes

  • Repeatable measurement
  • Reliable data capture
  • Participant usability
  • Promising physiological endpoints
  • Promising participant-experience variables
  • Hypotheses for subsequent investigation

Scope boundary

The pilot is not intended to establish clinical efficacy, diagnose medical conditions or validate therapeutic claims.

Sophisticated acoustic analysis and subsequent controlled studies sit beyond the immediate October phase. Detailed hardware and software implementation will be managed within research platform development.

A continuing research collaboration

Future studies can build on the Shobotics research platform and the jointly owned study data, while changing the sound protocol, questionnaire, participant cohort, physiological endpoints and analysis methodology.

A subsequent phase can introduce control conditions, standardized sound protocols, larger cohorts and formal statistical hypotheses once the October dataset identifies the most informative measurements.

The longer-term opportunity

Give Svaram a practical research infrastructure through which its experiences can generate evidence over time.

Proposed next step

Confirm the October pilot, research roles and ownership principles. Freeze the study protocol and questionnaire, and prepare for participant collection after the early-October dry runs.

References & planning basis

  1. Validity of the Polar H10 for heart rate variability and cardiac autonomic reflex tests, Autonomic Neuroscience (2026). Supports the physiological sensor rationale; the reported population was healthy young adults.
  2. The participant questionnaire will be developed for this study. Final wording, experience domains and scoring will be agreed during protocol development.
